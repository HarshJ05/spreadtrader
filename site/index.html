Trading the gap between related futures, such as WTI vs Brent crude, gold vs silver or 5-year vs 10-year Treasuries. When a gap stretches unusually far, the engine bets it will close. Every trade below happened on data the model had not yet seen.

How it works

  1. Pick pairs for a reason. 11 pairs with an economic link (same commodity, refined products, neighbouring points on the yield curve). Pairs are not chosen by searching for the best backtest.
  2. Walk forward. Every 6 months, look back 2 years and run an Engle-Granger cointegration test. A pair is only traded in the next 6 months if its spread kept returning to its average (p < 0.05) with a half-life between 1 and 60 days.
  3. Track the hedge ratio. A Kalman filter updates how many units of one future hedge the other, every day, using only past prices. Its prediction error is the spread; comparing it with its own previous 6 months gives a z-score (how many normal-sized moves from fair value).
  4. Trade the stretch. Sell the spread when z > 2, buy it when z < −2, exit when it comes back inside ±0.5. Stop out beyond ±4 or after 3 half-lives. Decisions at today's close earn tomorrow's return.
  5. Compare honestly. Same rules with a classic static hedge ratio, against buying and holding S&P 500 futures, after costs.

Limits. Prices are Yahoo Finance continuous front-month futures, which jump when contracts roll; those jumps are not adjusted. Returns are per unit of notional with no leverage or margin. WTI's negative settlement on 20 April 2020 is removed because a log price does not exist there. This is a research backtest, not trading advice.
